### Summary

The catalogue's machine-readable form is in good shape — 19 components, symmetric dependency edges, an acyclic graph, single-owned entities, and infrastructure correctly confined to `external_dependencies` — and all four sensors pass. But three Critical findings block readiness: the error-code vocabulary that `stories.md` explicitly assigns to this stage is owned by nobody (R-01), the advisory inline collision lookup is given the write path's `IMMEDIATE` transaction and would take a write lock on the single shared SQLite file on every form blur (R-02), and the five-dimension filter cannot express the directory's holder search or the holder-row link, leaving two released behaviours with no owning component and breaking ADR-003's "none of them counts a count of its own" (R-03). Three Major findings add a missing deletion-refusal owner (R-04), a mermaid diagram that contradicts the catalogue it derives from and the file's own acyclicity claim (R-05), and two unstated cross-cutting obligations — the connection-level `busy_timeout` no component owns and an unsupported `ActorResolver → DomainVocabulary` edge (R-06). This is an advisory pass, so the verdict informs the approval gate rather than blocking it: these are the findings a human should weigh before approving, and R-01 to R-03 in particular are cheap to fix in the catalogue now and expensive to fix in code later.
