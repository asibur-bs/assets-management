### What is fit for the customer

The three approved invariants are stated as testable requirements rather than as prose, and each carries a pass/fail a checker can actually run: append-only history is enforced by the database's own triggers (FR2.7) rather than by application code, the five buckets are mutually exclusive and exhaustive (FR3.1, FR6.3), and the four count-consuming surfaces are required to compute from one shared definition (FR6.1) and to ship as one indivisible group (FR6.8). Audit Completeness — the release's primary success measure — has a defined population, a defined predicate, and a defined exclusion rule, and the exclusion is the `system` actor rather than a timestamp or a flag, so it is measurable against a countable whole table. The actor-trust rule is specified as a rule and not as a mechanism: the mechanism stays deferred behind a named next action (OQ1), while the constraints on any mechanism — trusted server-side resolution, a request-supplied actor ignored rather than rejected, no client-settable field or header as the source — are already binding (FR2.2, FR2.3, NFR2.1, NFR2.5). A user-facing consequence is that the operator never sees a transient lock condition as a failure, because the busy condition carries its own retryable code (FR1.6a, FR1.7, NFR3.2) distinct from both the duplicate refusal and a generic internal error.

The lifecycle is complete for the eight released edges and explicitly bounded at ten: the two deferred discard edges are recorded as approved edges with their standing intact, in a boundary section that keeps "deferred" and "rejected" visibly apart, so a later reader is not misled about whether they are coming or gone. The two recovery-related behaviours that most often get lost — the distinct `recovered` action and the inline resolution of an assignment on discard — are pinned in FR3.7 and FR3.9 with their own pass/fail clauses, and the previous-holder preservation is specified as a write the transition performs, not as a field that happens to be there. The migration ordering constraint, the one thing in this release whose failure is silent, is a requirement with an evaluable pass/fail rather than an assumption, and the artifact that carries it says so in binding language.

For the operator, the duplicate-detection experience is specified end to end: an advisory inline warning that is dismissible and never writes, and a submit-time block that is the write's authority, offers exactly two next steps, and offers no override. The CSV export carries a fixed six-column set, is uncapped, never silently truncates, produces a header-only file on an empty filter, and prefixes every string cell that a spreadsheet would read as a formula.

### Carried forward

It is a precision issue in FR1.7's new duplicate-serial wording, not a scope or contract defect, and it does not block approval — a builder who reads FR1.5 alongside FR1.7 gets the right answer, but the two requirements should not be left to be read together to be reconcilable.
