### What is fit for the customer

The three approved invariants are stated as testable requirements rather than as prose, each with a pass/fail a checker can actually run: append-only history is enforced by the database's own triggers rather than by application code (FR2.7), the five buckets are mutually exclusive and exhaustive (FR3.1, FR6.3), and the four count-consuming surfaces compute from one shared definition (FR6.1) and ship as one indivisible group (FR6.8). Audit Completeness — the release's primary success measure — has a defined population, a defined predicate and a defined exclusion rule, and the exclusion is the `system` actor rather than a timestamp or a flag, so it is measurable against a countable whole table.

The actor-trust rule is specified as a rule and not as a mechanism: the mechanism stays deferred behind a named next action (OQ1), while the constraints on any mechanism are already binding. The user-facing consequence is that the operator never sees a transient lock condition as a failure, because the busy condition carries its own retryable code (FR1.6a, FR1.7, NFR3.2) distinct from both the duplicate refusal and a generic internal error.

The lifecycle is complete for the eight released edges and explicitly bounded at ten: the two deferred discard edges are recorded as approved edges with their standing intact, in a boundary section that keeps "deferred" and "rejected" visibly apart. The two recovery-related behaviours most often lost — the distinct `recovered` action and the inline resolution of an assignment on discard — are pinned in FR3.7 and FR3.9 with their own pass/fail clauses, and previous-holder preservation is specified as a write the transition performs rather than a field that happens to be there. The migration ordering constraint, the one thing in this release whose failure is silent, is a requirement with an evaluable pass/fail rather than an assumption.

For the operator, the duplicate-detection experience is specified end to end, and R-12's fix is what makes that true in the written contract rather than only in the prose around it: the inline warning and the submit-time block depend on the application-level predicate existing, and FR1.7 now says so in the same requirement that carries the error codes. A duplicate caught by the pre-check and a duplicate caught by the index now surface the same code, so the administrator sees one consistent refusal whether the collision was caught early or caught at the database — and the CSV export carries a fixed six-column set, is uncapped, never silently truncates, produces a header-only file on an empty filter, and prefixes every string cell a spreadsheet would read as a formula.
