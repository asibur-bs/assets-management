### Summary

The substance of this intent is in good shape and the R-15 fix is correctly implemented in both of its parts: the design-system enumeration is now explicitly indicative with workbook section 11 named as authoritative, and the `[memory:M2]` rule is split so that it remains usable as a citation target. I re-verified all fifteen prior findings against the current bytes and all fifteen hold — R-01 through R-14 substantively, and R-15 as resolved. The claim-sources, required-sections and upstream-coverage sensors all pass on both deliverables.

Four findings are new. One of them (R-16) is the reason the verdict is NOT-READY rather than READY: the stage's own required-section list names an **Initiative Trigger** section, and it is absent from `intent-statement.md` — Q4's trigger answer is registered in the artifact's source register but never stated as a claim. The `required-sections` sensor cannot catch this, because with no template bound to the stage it degrades to a generic at-least-two-H2 floor, which the project's own recorded correction identifies as the exact blind spot this situation falls into. The remaining three (R-17 heading names, R-18 `[desc]` provenance precision, R-19 deliverable-level register completeness) are Minor and do not block; each is a fidelity or navigability point rather than a wrong product claim.

None of the new findings concern the product decisions themselves. The scope boundary, the standing conditions, the actor-trust rule, the amendment surface, the remainder feature set and the stakeholder identifications all remain traceable to their approved upstream sources, and no unselected option has been turned into a requirement or an exclusion. The verdict turns on a missing deliverable section, not on a disagreement with the product call.
