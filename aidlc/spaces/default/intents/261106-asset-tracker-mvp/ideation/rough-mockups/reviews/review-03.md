### Summary

This round the five previously-raised findings are genuinely resolved, and I verified each one against the current bytes rather than taking the prior list on trust. The dashboard arithmetic reconciles (37 + 191 + 9 + 6 + 5 = 248 = Total Records; 248 − 5 = 243 = Active Inventory; W2's "Showing 248 of 248" and F6's export row count follow from the same shared counting function). The F5a transition map is correct: I traced all eight connectors column by column, and every arrowhead lands on its stated destination — `returned → lost` climbs the right-hand riser at column 113 and terminates with a junction mark in the `lost` box's top wall, and the Recover edge runs leftwards and terminates at `in-stock`, not at `assigned`. The F1 inline branch terminates at "W3 Asset detail — the EXISTING asset" and never joins the create-success path, with a defined dismiss-and-continue exit that still reaches the authoritative submit-time check. The display-holder rule is now stated correctly and the Unassigned row's chips and row total follow from it. The CSV rationale names only the six exported columns. The F5f write-flow enumeration covers all eight write flows and the audit vocabulary names all seven actions. The ten-edge reconciliation is stated identically and arithmetically correctly in both documents, and neither omitted edge has been withdrawn from the approved decision set. Scope fidelity is clean: every mention of the design system, responsive UI, improved search, pagination, or empty states is a deferral, and the design-system enumeration stays indicative rather than exhaustive.

What I could not clear is a different class of defect: internal contradictions between the two deliverables and between a frame and the prose that describes it. Five of them are material. The F4 flow frame draws three rows for Dana Reyes while labelling her "4 assets" with chips "[Assigned 3] [Lost 1]" — the frame contradicts the chips, the W6 frame for the same holder, and the F4 prose beside it, which says "Dana's row lists four assets". The W3 asset-detail frame offers "Assign to [ ] [Assign]" on an asset whose own chip reads `[Assigned]`, contradicting the document's own actions table, which gives an `assigned` asset only Mark returned, Mark lost and Discard — and the approved transition table has no `assigned → assigned` edge, so a developer building from that frame would render a control the service must reject. Both deliverables' "Sources Consumed" sections state that the stage's question file holds "the seven confirmed answers and the four accepted assumptions" when it holds nineteen and thirty-three respectively, which understates this stage's own decision record in the very artifact set that exists to be auditable. The dialog submit buttons are named differently in the two documents, and the wireframes make the label a deliberate specified choice. And the recovery transition's effect on the two holder fields is never stated, which matters because the display-holder rule and the Unassigned row both depend on it.

None of these are matters of taste; each is checkable against the artifacts' own bytes. Together they mean a developer reading only one of the two documents, or only one of the frames, would implement something different from what the other document says — which is the defect class this initiative exists to remove, reproduced inside the specification of the fix. I am returning NOT-READY on that basis. The fixes are localised and none of them reopens an approved decision, so the stage should be close to READY on the next pass.
