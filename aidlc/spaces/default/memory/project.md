- The fully approved 17-item Asset Tracker MVP decision set, together with the Asset Tracker MVP Development Workbook, is the binding technical and product contract for the `asset-tracker-mvp` intent; it is not reopened at any stage of that intent, and it carries seven standing conditions — the uniqueness check uses an IMMEDIATE transaction and a non-zero SQLite `busy_timeout` with `SQLITE_BUSY` mapped to a retryable error; the functional-index expression and the application-level comparison predicate use one shared definition; registration and serial/key updates enforce the same uniqueness policy; historical backfills preserve original records and audit traceability and report failures; dashboard, assignee, asset-list, and CSV counts use consistent filtering and counting definitions; every lifecycle transition has explicit validation and audit behaviour; and the existing 66/66 passing test baseline is preserved with regression tests added for newly approved behaviour. Traceability must be maintained between the approved MVP requirements and the POC requirements FR1.3, FR4.4, FR4.5, and FR5.3, and actor identity is resolved from trusted server-side context so actor information supplied in a write request is never trusted. (Stage intent-capture, 2026-11-06)
- The decision set's amendment table names the workbook amendment surface as section 4.1 (transition table, recovery, `in-stock → lost`, `assigned → discarded`), section 4.2.5, section 4.3 (the actor-trust rule), section 4.4, section 6.1, section 6.4, section 6.5, section 9 (Screens 01, 02, 03, 04, 10), section 10, section 21.1, section 21.2, section 22, section 24 (the metric qualifiers the Audit Completeness population relies on), and the two new section 27 sections (Inventory Counting Definitions and Audit Action Vocabulary). The section 24 amendment carries the Audit Completeness qualifier verbatim: pre-MVP history is backfilled to a `system` actor and excluded from this metric. Section 4.1 records the `lost → in-stock` recovery transition with a required recovery reason drawn from the shared loss-reason vocabulary, and requires a distinct `recovered` audit action rather than `assign` or `return`, whose description names the recovery and the prior holder. It also records that complex recovery workflows — partial recovery, credit assignment, dispute handling — remain deferred, distinct from the single included recovery transition. The workbook's remaining MVP feature set is the reconciliation dashboard, the assignee directory, asset editing, duplicate-detection UX, empty states, result counts, pagination, improved search, responsive UI, and a design system. The design-system enumeration is indicative, not exhaustive: workbook section 11 is the authoritative source for the complete design-system requirements, covering semantic color tokens, a typography scale, spacing tokens, radius and shadow scales, iconography, and a light-theme implementation designed for future dark-theme extension. (Stage intent-capture, 2026-11-06)
- The `assigned → discarded` transition resolves the assignment inline and records it: `assigned_to` is cleared, `last_assigned_to` is retained so the holder at discard time stays visible, and a `discarded` audit action records the discard, the prior holder, and the fact that the assignment was resolved by the discard. A discard reason is always required, drawn from the shared loss-reason vocabulary plus retirement-specific values. The asset row and its full history are never deleted; a discarded asset is excluded from Active Inventory but remains present in the `discarded` bucket and in Total Records, and stays accessible at its detail URL with its history intact. (Stage intent-capture, 2026-11-06)
